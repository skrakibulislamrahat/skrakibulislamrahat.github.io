About

My work sits at the intersection of medical imaging, machine learning, and deployment-aware AI evaluation. Instead of treating benchmark accuracy as the finish line, I focus on whether model behavior is clinically meaningful, stable under shift, and interpretable enough to justify trust.

A major part of my recent research is centered on diabetic retinopathy screening, especially how fundus models fail when they learn dataset artifacts, overconfident calibration maps, or shortcuts that do not reflect disease. I also work on multimodal systems that combine images with structured clinical variables, and on applied healthcare prediction problems including kidney disease, breast cancer, pneumonia, Parkinson’s disease, and heart disease.

Systematic Evidence of Artifact-Driven Shortcut Learning in Fundus Image Models

Structured analysis of how fundus models exploit borders, padding, and acquisition artifacts instead of pathology-relevant cues, with external validation on Messidor-2 and Grad-CAM auditing.

Under ReviewCMIGCalibration

Calibration Under Domain Shift in Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

Five-seed evaluation of temperature scaling transfer from APTOS to Messidor-2, showing that source-fitted calibration fails under shift while limited target-side calibration restores reliability.

Multimodal Deep Learning for Classifying Diabetic Retinopathy Severity

Explainable multimodal framework combining fundus images and structured clinical metadata, with cross-validation, external validation, Grad-CAM, and SHAP analysis.
